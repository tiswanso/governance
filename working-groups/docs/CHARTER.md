# AGNTCY Documentation Working Group Charter

## Mission

- To contribute to and set standards for writing, reviewing, organizing, maintaining, and publishing the AGNTCY collective's
documentation.
- To make high-quality documentation a part of AGNTCY’s components, helping contributors and users to understand, adopt, and extend AGNTCY components.


## Goals

Create a first-class documentation experience for AGNTCY users by:

- Establishing a documentation strategy and aligning new contributions with it
- Defining style guidelines and terminology for the doc set
- Coordinating the efforts of documentation contributors and encouraging new contributions
- Adopting appropriate tooling to help with automation
- Creating processes that:
  - Lower the time to address PRs and issues
  - Protect the quality of the documentation, especially over time
- Contributing to overall product direction through our experience as the earliest Knative users
- Ensure that documentation across AGNTCY projects is accurate, clear, discoverable, consistent, and sustainable.
- Serve as a coordinating body for cross-project doc efforts, including onboarding guides, tutorials, API references, governance docs, and community contributing docs.


## Scope
OG
This group will primarily concern itself with the content of the [docs](https://github.com/agntcy/docs) and [website](https://github.com/agntcy/agntcy-website) repos.

Proposed
- Coordinating cross-project documentation alignment (e.g. consistent layout, navigation, cross referencing, component reuse).
- Reviewing major documentation contributions (e.g. architecture overviews, protocol specs, design docs) for consistency with standards.
- Conducting periodic audits of existing docs to identify gaps, outdated content, broken links, or inconsistencies.
- Proposing documentation “improvement sprints” or hackathons, training sessions, or community events.
- Liaising with other WGs (Core, Identity, Observability, etc.) to ensure they abide by the documentation guidelines and integrate doc expectations into their roadmaps.
- Maintaining transparency: publishing meeting notes, decisions, and backlog publicly.

## Out of Scope
Out of Scope (Exclusions)
The DocWG does not:
- Author all documentation’s content (individual project maintainers remain responsible for their content’s domain correctness).
- Enforce technical policy or architecture decisions (that remains within the purview of technical WGs).
- Unilaterally enforce tooling changes without community consultation or coordination with other WGs.
- Manage project-specific codebase docs that are tightly coupled to internal APIs (though it may provide review or guidance).

## Deliverables & Metrics
Typical deliverables and success metrics include:
- A published Documentation Standards & Style Guide (markdown / web site).
- Tooling proposals and vetted decisions (e.g. selecting a static site generator, search, doc linter).
- Quarterly documentation health reports (coverage, stale content, link breaks).
- Audit & backlog of documentation gaps/issues, with assigned owners and progress tracking.
- Doc “onboarding guides” or “getting started” tutorials for new contributors.
- Community engagement metrics (PRs merged, reviews, contributions to docs).

## Decision Making & Process
The DocWG strives for consensus. If consensus cannot be reached, objections are documented, alternative proposals surfaced, and the sponsoring governance body (or parent WG) may arbitrate.
- Proposals (e.g. for tooling, standard changes) must be published in the open (e.g. issue or RFC), open a comment period, and then the DocWG votes (or signals consensus) after discussion.
- Meeting agendas, minutes, and decisions are to be published publicly.
- Changes to this charter itself should go through the same proposal + comment + consensus process.
  
## Membership & Roles
-  Membership is open to anyone in the AGNTCY community who expresses interest and commits to contributing (e.g. through doc review, writing, tooling).
Core membership (active members) should be individuals who regularly attend meetings, help drive work, review PRs, etc. The DocWG may maintain a roster or membership list, with roles (e.g. maintainers, reviewers, tool leads).
-  Roles & Responsibilities- Chair(s)/Co-Chairs — Facilitates meetings, sets agenda, coordinates with other WGs, ensures follow-up.
Documentation Leads / Area Owners — Oversee specific documentation areas (e.g. API reference, tutorials, architecture, onboarding).
Tooling Lead(s) — Drive evaluation, adoption, integration of doc tools and infrastructure.
- Reviewers / Maintainers — Responsible for critiquing documentation PRs, ensuring alignment with standards, mentoring contributors.
- Term & Rotation- Chairs and leads may serve defined terms (e.g. 1 year), with the option to re-nominate or rotate.
- If a member becomes inactive (e.g. misses >3 meetings in a row without notice), membership may be reviewed and potentially removed.

## Meetings & Logistics
- The DocWG meets weekly as decided by the group.
- Meeting notifications, agenda, and minutes are published in advance.
-Meetings allow for remote attendance (e.g. via video or voice) and be recorded or documented.
- The working group maintains a public backlog (e.g. GitHub issues or a board) for tracking tasks, proposals, and improvements.

## Communication & Transparency
- All DocWG discussions, proposals, and minutes should occur in public forums (GitHub, Slack, mailing list) so the wider AGNTCY community can observe and participate.
- Decisions and rationale should be documented and accessible (e.g. linked from /governance or /doc).
- Mechanism for appeals: community members may raise objections or ask for reconsideration of decisions, triggering review or discussion in the DocWG or escalation to governance.

## Charter Revision & Sunset
- This charter should be reviewed at least annually (or after major reorganizations) to verify relevance and propose updates.
Any revision to this charter must follow the proposal → comment → consensus process outlined above.
- If at any time the DocWG becomes inactive or redundant, it may be sunset by a governance decision, with responsibilities folded into a successor body or via direct project ownership.
